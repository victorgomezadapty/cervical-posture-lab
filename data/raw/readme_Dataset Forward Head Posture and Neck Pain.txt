General description: the data of 92 participants (50 with chronic neck pain and 42 asymptomatic) was collected to a) determine whether significant differences in head posture, assessed through the craniovertebral angle (CVA) exist between adults with and without chronic neck pain; b)compare the posture by sex, age BMI, and physical activity; c) assess reliability of craniovertebral angle between two evaluators.

METHODOLOGICAL INFORMATION
--------------------------

Description of methods used for collection/generation of data: 
The study was disseminated through social networks, posters and lectures in Faculty of Physiotherapy and School of Architecture at the University of A Coruna between April-May 2021 and February-March 2022. Data were collected through an online form and photogrammetry method.
 
Software- or Instrument-specific information needed to interpret the data, including software and hardware version numbers:  SPSS version 29.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, Illinois, USA)
  
The statistical analysis includes:
Descriptive analyses were conducted for all study variables. Categorical data were reported as frequencies and percentages, and continuous data as mean and standard deviation (SD) or medians, as appropriate. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilks test and histograms inspection. Group differences (chronic neck pain vs asymptomatic) were analyzed using the Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, depending on distribution. For the craniovertebral angle, mean differences and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Effect sizes were estimated using Cohen’s d (based on pooled SD), and interpreted as small (d = 0.2), medium (d = 0.5), or large (d ≥ 0.8). Exploratory analyses included comparison by sex (female vs. male), age (18–24 years vs. >24 years), BMI (normal vs. overweight/obese), and moderate physical activity (above vs. below median). In the chronic neck pain group, association between craniovertebral angle and symptoms (pain intensity and disability) was explored. Spearman’s Rho was used to assess correlations between quantitative variables (e.g., craniovertebral angle in sitting vs. standing). Craniovertebral angle reliability was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) based on a two-way random effect model with absolute agreement (single rater/measurement).  ICC were interpreted following Landis and Koch. A bilateral approach was maintained, with p<0.05 being considered statistically significant. 
  
Environmental/experimental conditions:
A two-group cross-sectional, controlled, and assessor-blind study was conducted at the University of A Coruna (UDC), Northwest Spain, between April-May 2021 and February-March 2022. The study compared participants with chronic neck pain to asymptomatic individuals. After being informed about the study objectives, participants voluntarily completed the required data collection, questionnaires and physical assessment of head posture (photogrammetry) for the research.
